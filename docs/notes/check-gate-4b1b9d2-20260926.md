## Corrected judgment, recorded on evidence

`TT_close_guard_off` drops the `if !*closed` guard so a second `close()` shuts
the socket down again. I first judged this a **real gap**, expecting the repeated
`shutdown` to fail. Measured instead: on Linux a repeated `shutdown(Both)` still
returns `Ok`, so the two variants are indistinguishable from outside the
endpoint. It is recorded as **equivalent on the measurement**, not as a kill and
not on my expectation. The scratch experiment was removed afterwards and the
worktree restored before the final probe.

`TT_send_no_flush` was likewise measured, not argued: with the mutant applied a
4096-byte frame still arrives whole, and std confirms it - `Write::flush` for
both `TcpStream` and `&TcpStream` is literally `Ok(())`, because a TCP socket
holds no userspace buffer.

## The other two equivalences

- `TN_loopback_client`: the listener binds `Ipv4Addr::LOCALHOST` and the client
  connects to that address, so the client's local address is necessarily
  loopback whenever the accepted peer address is; the remaining clauses cover it.
- `TE_addr_poison`: renaming the error in the poisoned-mutex `map_err` of
  `local_addr`. Nothing panics while holding the stream mutex (the only other
  holder is `close`, whose body is two infallible calls plus `?`), so the mutex
  cannot be poisoned and the closure never runs.
