## The trap the probe caught

This line has **two emitters** - `failover_server` and `failover_client` - and they
produce **byte-identical text**. My first attempt added the full-line assertion to the
two tests whose logs I had, and **both passed**. But **three mutants on the CLIENT
emitter SURVIVED**: an assertion on identical text is satisfied by whichever emitter's
occurrence is in that log, and both of my logs happened to carry only the **SERVER's**
line. A passing assertion was therefore **not** evidence that the client emitter was
covered.

The fix required finding a test that captures the failover **client's** stdout and
shows it carries that line -
`udp_listener_rejects_bounded_malformed_churn_then_authenticates_and_cleans_up` does
(its `out.stdout` also carries `failover_client_ok`) - and asserting there. With that,
all three client mutants and all four server mutants are killed.

**Reusable lesson:** when one output text has multiple emitters, a text-based
assertion cannot tell you which emitter produced it. Identical text makes the two
indistinguishable by content alone, so coverage of each emitter must be established on
a log known to come from that emitter - and only a per-emitter mutant proves it.
